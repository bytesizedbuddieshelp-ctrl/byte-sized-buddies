## A 45-minute lesson

Every lesson follows the same shape, so you always know what comes next.

1. **Welcome and a warm-up question.** Three minutes.
2. **Learn it.** Show the slides. One idea on each slide. About 10 minutes.
3. **Try it.** Guided practice on each person's own device. About 20 minutes.
4. **Worksheet and teach-back.** Each learner shows a neighbor what they learned. Seven minutes.
5. **Keep it.** Hand out the large-print handout and explain the take-home challenge. Five minutes.

## What to bring

Print our [visit-day checklist](/visit-checklist) to tick things off as you go.

- A large-print handout and worksheet for every learner.
- The slideshow, on a laptop. A TV or projector helps if the room has one.
- A phone or tablet signed in to a practice account, so you can show each step.
- A friend. Two teachers can help more people at once.

## How to run a hands-on class

- Teach one idea at a time. Show it once, then let everyone try it.
- Walk around while people practice. Sit beside a learner instead of standing over them.
- Say what will happen next. "A video will pop up. That's normal."
- Normalize mistakes. "Everyone gets stuck here. Let's try again."
- Use short sentences and everyday words. Explain any technical word the first time you say it.
- Say "older adults" or use people's names.
- When you can, practice on the learner's own device. That is what they will use at home.

## The scam-safety rule

Every lesson includes a calm reminder, never a scare. Teach it as three steps:

1. Hang up. Or don't click.
2. Call the number on your card.
3. Call someone you trust.

Never ask learners to type a real password in class. Use demo accounts or a practice screen.

## Photos and video

Take photos or video of learners only with written consent from the person and from the facility. If you do not have consent, do not take the picture. Screenshots of a practice screen are always fine.
