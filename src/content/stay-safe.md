---
title: Stay safe online
---

A few small habits keep you safe when a call, text, or message feels strange. This page is for learners, families, and staff. You can print it and keep it by the phone.

## The three-step rule

If anything feels strange, do these three things, in this order.

1. **Hang up.** Or don't tap. You never have to answer right away.
2. **Call the number on your card.** Use the number printed on your bank card, your bill, or your insurance card. Never use a number someone gave you.
3. **Call someone you trust.** A family member, a friend, or a staff member. Say what happened.

You can always take a minute. Nothing real needs an answer this second.

## Phone calls and texts

- A real bank or company will not ask for your password, your PIN, or a code sent to your phone.
- Gift cards are never a way to pay a bill, a fine, or a fee.
- A caller who says "act now" or "don't tell anyone" is a reason to hang up.
- If a text says you owe money or missed a delivery, don't tap the link. Open the company's own app, or call the number on your card.
- You can let unknown numbers go to voicemail. A real caller will leave a message.

## Email and messages

- Look at who the message is really from. A friend's name can be copied by someone else.
- Don't tap a link or open an attachment you did not expect. If it seems to be from a friend, call them and ask.
- If a message is about an account, open that company's app or website yourself. Don't use the link in the message.

## Pop-ups and warnings

A box that says your device is broken, or has a virus, and gives a phone number, is a trick. Close it. Do not call the number. If it won't close, turn the device off and on again, and ask someone you trust to look.

## Passwords

- Never tell a password or a code to someone who calls you.
- In our lessons, we never ask learners to type a real password. We use practice accounts.
- Ask someone you trust to help you set up a safe place to keep your passwords.

## If you already tapped, shared, or paid

Everyone makes mistakes. It is not your fault, and it can happen to anyone.

1. Call your bank, using the number on your card. Tell them what happened.
2. Tell someone you trust.
3. Change the password for any account you shared. Ask for help if you need it.

## For families and staff

- Talk about scams the way you would talk about the weather: calmly and often. Fear makes people hide mistakes. Calm makes them ask.
- Agree on a short word or phrase with your family. Anyone who calls in an emergency can use it.
- Invite questions. "Did you get a strange call this week?" is a good way to start.
- Our free lessons teach these habits in a hands-on way. Ask for a visit, or teach them yourself.
